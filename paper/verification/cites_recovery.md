## 1. Aho & Peterson 1972 — VERIFIED-ABSTRACT (full text paywalled)

- Crossref confirmed: SIAM Journal on Computing 1(4):305–312, Dec 1972, DOI 10.1137/0201022. (confirmed via api.crossref.org)
- Full text: SIAM epubs is paywalled; curl returned Cloudflare HTML; no legal OA mirror found (Semantic Scholar: CLOSED; OpenAlex: no OA location; archive.org: nothing).
- Abstract fetched from https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0201022 (via WebFetch page-extract; identical wording independently returned by a second search-engine snippet, so I treat it as verbatim):

  > "We assume three types of syntax errors can debase the sentences of a language generated by a context-free grammar: the replacement of a symbol by an incorrect symbol, the insertion of an extraneous symbol, or the deletion of a symbol." (Abstract)

  > "We present an algorithm that will parse any input string to completion finding the fewest possible number of errors. On a random access computer the algorithm requires time proportional to the cube of the length of the input." (Abstract)

- Claim check: cubic time — SUPPORTED (abstract). Insertion/deletion/substitution edits — SUPPORTED (abstract; their word for substitution is "replacement"). Global minimum-distance — SUPPORTED ("fewest possible number of errors").
- **"Covering grammar with error productions": NOT verified from the primary text** (I could not access the body). Contemporaneous secondary support, verbatim from Lyon 1974 (which I do hold, CACM 17(1) p. 3):
  > "Peterson, 1972 [25, 26], develops a theoretical least-errors parser using Earley's algorithm and extended grammars. New rules supplement an original grammar to account for insertion, deletion, and mutation errors." (Lyon 1974, p. 3)
  If you keep the phrase "covering grammar with error productions," it matches the standard description (also in Diekmann & Tratt §2) but I could not quote it from Aho–Peterson themselves.

## 3. Burke & Fisher 1987 — VERIFIED-FULLTEXT

- Crossref: ACM TOPLAS 9(2):164–197, 1987 (Crossref print date 1987-03-20), DOI 10.1145/22719.22720. Full text: ACM bronze OA PDF (pdfs/burke.pdf).
- Quotes:
  > "It employs a new technique, parse action deferral, that allows the most appropriate recovery in cases where this would ordinarily be precluded by late detection of the error." (Abstract, p. 164)

  > "This modification may take the form of the insertion or deletion of a single token, the substitution of one token for another, or the merging of two tokens into one. Such a single token repair shall be referred to as a simple repair" (p. 166)

  > "a parse check determines how many tokens in the right context of the error token can be consumed before blocking on an error." (p. 171)

  > "The analysis is thus divided into simple, scope, and secondary recovery." (p. 166) — secondary recovery: "A secondary recovery consists in discarding text that precedes, follows, or surrounds the error token." (p. 166)

Scope/phrase-level recovery as fallback — SUPPORTED (scope + secondary recovery; note their term is "scope"/"secondary," not "phrase-level" — "phrase-level" is the textbook family name, fine as a gloss but not their word).

## Summary of discrepancies found

1. **Medeiros follow-up: DOI 10.1002/spe.2789 is a different, unrelated paper** (Ghazouani et al., SPE 50(4), multicloud services). Correct: Science of Computer Programming 187:102373 (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.scico.2019.102373, with third author Gilney de Azevedo Alvez Junior. The paper is titled "Automatic syntax error reporting and recovery in parsing expression grammars," not "Automatic insertion of error recovery expressions."
2. Lyon: keep subtitle "A Practical Approach" (on the PDF title page; Crossref drops it). Time bound is cubic (space quadratic) — don't cite it as quadratic time.
3. Aho–Peterson: "covering grammar with error productions" could not be quoted from the primary text (paywalled); abstract supports cubic time + three edit types + global minimum.
4. Diekmann–Tratt: "cannot revise input before the error point" is an accurate characterization but not a sentence in the paper — don't put it in quotes.
5. Burke–Fisher: their terms are "simple/scope/secondary recovery"; "phrase-level" is a gloss.
6. de Jonge et al.: TOPLAS 34(4) Article 15 (15:1–15:50); quotes verified against the TUD-SERG-2012-021 preprint, not the ACM-paginated version.
7. Levenshtein: English translation Feb 1966, Soviet Physics Doklady 10(8):707–710; Russian original Doklady AN SSSR 163(4):845–848 (1965) — confirmed from the scan itself.
