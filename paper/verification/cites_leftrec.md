## 1. Warth, Douglass, Millstein 2008 — "Packrat Parsers Can Support Left Recursion"

**Status: VERIFIED-FULLTEXT**
- Metadata: Crossref `10.1145/1328408.1328424` — PEPM '08 proceedings, ACM, pp. 103–110, published 2008-01-07. Authors/affiliations confirmed (Warth: VPRI/UCLA; Douglass: Boeing; Millstein: UCLA).
- Full text: https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~todd/research/pepm08.pdf (author copy; local file `warth2008.pdf`). Paper front matter: "This paper is also available as VPRI Technical Report TR-2007-002." (vpri.org itself did not resolve from this machine.)

### Quotes
1. Seed-growing (§3.2, p. 105): "We refer to this iterative process as growing the seed; Figure 3 shows GROW-LR, which implements the seed-growing algorithm."
2. LR/heads/involved-rule bookkeeping (§3.4, p. 106) — the paper's data-type declarations, verbatim: "LR : (seed : AST, rule : RULE, head : HEAD, next : LR)" and "HEAD : (rule : RULE, involvedSet, evalSet : SET of RULE)". Also (§3.3, p. 106): "We refer to the rule that started that loop as the head rule of the left recursion, and to the other rules in the loop as being involved in that left recursion."
3. Complexity admission (§5, p. 108): "Our iterative process for growing the seed parse of a left-recursive application violates this assumption, thus making it possible for some left-recursive grammars to yield super-linear parse times."

### On associativity — nuance for the paper's claim
- What Warth et al. actually say: left recursion "is typically used to express the syntax of left-associative operators" (§1, p. 103), and re left-recursion elimination: "a suitable transformation must preserve the leftassociativity of the parse trees generated by the resulting non-leftrecursive rule" (§1, p. 104).
- **Discrepancy to note:** the paper says NOTHING about grammars mixing left and right recursion (E <- E "-" E). It does not "treat ambiguous-associativity" at all — it is silent. The finding that their algorithm yields a right-associative (right-biased) parse for such rules is due to Tratt 2010 (§5) and Medeiros et al. 2014 (§4), not to Warth et al. themselves.
- **Discrepancy on "the paper itself admits bugs with indirect LR":** the paper does NOT admit bugs. Its only self-criticism is the super-linear-time admission quoted above. The "complex / only direct LR shipped in OMeta" admission is attributed by Medeiros et al. to a 2008 OMeta mailing-list post by Warth (Medeiros ref [21], http://vpri.org/pipermail/ometa/2008-June/000006.html), and the incorrectness findings are Tratt's and Medeiros's (see items 2, 3, 8).

## 2. Tratt 2010 — "Direct Left-Recursive Parsing Expression Grammars" (tech report EIS-10-01)

**Status: VERIFIED-FULLTEXT**
- Full text: https://tratt.net/laurie/research/pubs/papers/tratt__direct_left_recursive_parsing_expression_grammars.pdf (local `tratt2010.pdf`). Title page confirms: "Technical report EIS-10-01, Middlesex University, The Burroughs, London, NW4 4BT, United Kingdom. October 25, 2010." Author: Laurence Tratt.

### Quotes
1. The requested "violates" sentence (§5, p. 7): "However this parse violates a fundamental aspect of PEGs, which is that rules match text greedily in order to ensure unambiguous parses [4]." (Context: parsing 1-2-3 with `expr <- expr "-" expr / num` "we have obtained a right-associative parse.")
2. Warth's indirect-LR machinery is complicated; Tratt is direct-only (§3, p. 3): "Warth et al. first present a relatively simple adaption of Packrat parsing to cope with direct left-recursion, before detailing a much more complicated adaption to cope with indirect left-recursion." Followed immediately by: "direct recursion raises sufficiently many issues that I concentrate solely on it."
3. Warth incorrect for some PEGs (Abstract, p. 1): "I then demonstrate that this approach results in incorrect parses for some PEGs, before outlining a restrictive subset of left-recursive PEGs which can safely work with this algorithm."
   And (§5, p. 7): "exactly the same issue can be seen in faithful implementations of Warth et al.'s algorithm. Given the following PEG, Warth's OMeta tool [11] produces exactly the same incorrect parse for 1-2-3".
4. Direct-only confirmed again in Conclusion (§8, p. 12): "the obvious next step is to extend the solutions presented in this paper to deal with indirect left and indirect right recursion, this may well prove to be quite challenging".

All three stated claims check out (surprising/right associativity, problems traced to Warth's algorithm incl. its faithful implementations, direct-LR-only scope).

## 3. Medeiros, Mascarenhas, Ierusalimschy 2014 — "Left Recursion in Parsing Expression Grammars"

**Status: VERIFIED-FULLTEXT**
- Metadata: Crossref `10.1016/j.scico.2014.01.013` — Science of Computer Programming, vol. 96, pp. 177–190, Dec 2014, Elsevier, ISSN 0167-6423. Crossref title capitalizes "Parsing Expression Grammars".
- Full text read: arXiv:1207.0443v3 (13 Feb 2014), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0443 (local `medeiros2014.pdf`). arXiv API: submitted 2012-07-02, last revised 2014-02-13.

### Quotes
1. Bounded left recursion (§3, p. 8 of arXiv version): "Intuitively, bounded left recursion is a use of a non-terminal where we limit the number of left-recursive uses it may have. This is the basis of our extension for supporting left recursion in PEGs."
2. Iterative bound increase (§3, pp. 8–9): "it is sufficient to increase the bound until the size of the matched prefix stops increasing." And (Conclusion, §7): "we use an iterative process to find the smallest bound that gives the longest match for a particular use of the non-terminal."
3. Cost/complexity — **the paper states no asymptotic complexity bound for its left-recursion handling.** The only performance statement is (Conclusion, §7): "Preliminary benchmarks show that left-recursive grammars perform similarly to the same grammars with left recursion removed." Related caveat for packrat implementations (Related Work, §6/p. 20): "a left-recursive packrat parser cannot index the packrat memoization table just by a parsing expression and a subject, as the L table is also involved."

**Discrepancy to note:** if the paper draft claims Medeiros et al. state a specific complexity (e.g., linear or polynomial), that is unsupported — no such bound appears in the paper.

### Quotes

## 6. Hutchison 2020 — "Pika parsing..." (arXiv:2005.06444)

**Status: VERIFIED-FULLTEXT**
- Metadata: arXiv API — submitted 2020-05-13, v4 2020-07-07. Author: Luke A. D. Hutchison. PDF header: "arXiv:2005.06444v4 [cs.PL] 7 Jul 2020". Local `hutchison2020.pdf`. (Preprint styled for ACM TOPLAS but, per DBLP/arXiv, not published in a journal — cite as arXiv.)

### Quotes
1. Bottom-up right-to-left DP + LR (Abstract): "a novel reformulation of packrat parsing as a dynamic programming algorithm, which requires parsing the input in reverse: bottom-up and right to left ... enables pika parsers to handle grammars that use either direct or indirect left recursion".
2. Error recovery via designated rules (§3.2, p. 13): "Recovering after a syntax error involves finding the next match in the memo table after the end of the syntax error for any grammar rule of interest". (Fig. 5 caption area, §3.2: "any rules could be chosen for the purpose of identifying complete parses of rules of interest, and whatever is not spanned by those rules is treated as a syntax error.") — Note the paper's term is "rules of interest", not "recovery rules".
3. Overhead (Abstract): "a large constant performance impact was incurred per input character for the pika parser, which allowed Parboiled2 and ANTLR4 to perform significantly better than the pika parser for this grammar". Benchmarks (§6): "the pika parser is approximately 100 times slower than Parboiled2 for moderate to long input lengths". Conclusion-side framing (§6.5): "the overhead of pika parsing relative to packrat parsing may be acceptable, particularly where the benefits of direct handling of left recursion and optimal error recovery are worth the constant performance penalty".

## 8. Does anyone state Warth's algorithm is incorrect for some grammars? YES — both.

- **Tratt 2010** (Abstract): "I then demonstrate that this approach results in incorrect parses for some PEGs". (§5): "exactly the same issue can be seen in faithful implementations of Warth et al.'s algorithm. Given the following PEG, Warth's OMeta tool [11] produces exactly the same incorrect parse for 1-2-3".
- **Medeiros et al. 2014** (Related Work, p. 20 of arXiv v3): "Warth et al.'s algorithm also produces surprising results with some grammars, both directly and indirectly left-recursive, that are related to its reuse of the memoization table, as reported by some users of Warth et al.'s algorithm [23, 24, 25]." And, for the nullable-LR grammar S<-X; X<-XY/eps; Y<-x: "The algorithm fails to parse strings of the form x+, because the nullable left recursion of X will make the algorithm mistakenly fail the matching of the non-terminal Y that follows X". Also: "its full version, with support for indirect left recursion, is complex, as noted by the authors [21]. The released versions of the authors' PEG parsing library, OMeta [22], only implement support for direct left recursion to avoid the extra complexity [21]." (Medeiros ref [21] = Warth, OMeta mailing list, June 2008.)
- Bonus (Medeiros §4, p. 13): "The algorithm proposed by Warth et al. to handle left-recursion in PEGs [7] also has a right-recursive bias in rules that have both left and right-recursion, as noted by Tratt [8]."
